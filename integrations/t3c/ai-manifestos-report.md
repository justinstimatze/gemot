## Methodology Notes

**Agent construction**: Speaker-derived agents are grounded in T3C's extracted claims and speaker-crux matrix stances. Steelman clusters group speakers with ≥70% voting pattern similarity. Topic probes group cruxes by T3C topic and investigate deeper disagreements. Round 2 agents (bridge, dissent, empty chair) are informed by Round 1 analysis results.

**Score interpretation**: Crux scores reflect the proportion of *participating synthetic agents* that agree vs. disagree. With 5 speaker-derived and 6 structural agents, these scores indicate the *topology of the discourse*, not the strength of evidence or real-world expert consensus. Ordinal labels are used instead of raw percentages. Scores are reported separately for speaker-derived and structural agents.

**Multiple comparisons**: This analysis tests multiple cruxes simultaneously without correction for multiple comparisons. With 10+ cruxes across a small agent pool, some apparent disagreements may be artefacts of the generation process rather than genuine discourse splits. The LLM both generates crux claims and assigns agent stances — a self-referential loop that inflates apparent disagreement patterns.

**Circularity**: AI-synthesized agents deliberating about AI governance is inherently circular. This report maps discourse structure — it does not produce independent evidence. Conclusions should be treated as hypotheses to be tested against primary sources and real expert input.

**Replicability**: This is a single pipeline run. LLM outputs are stochastic — a second run on the same input will produce different crux wordings, scores, and topic labels. Findings that appear across multiple runs are more robust than single-run results.
